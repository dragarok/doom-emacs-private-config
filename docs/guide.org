#+TITLE: How this Emacs works
#+STARTUP: overview

Each second-level heading below is one tip.  The dashboard's *Tip* button
shows them one at a time, in order, and picks up where you left off.
*All tips* opens this file.

* Reminders on the phone

** A task with a time rings by itself
Give a task a time and the Emacs Home app rings it *10 minutes before*,
even when Emacs is asleep.  The notification has three buttons: *Open*,
*Clock in* and *Snooze*.

To give it a time, schedule it with =SPC m d s= and type a time, for
example =9:30=, =tomorrow 9am= or =fri 14:00=.  A deadline or a plain
timestamp with a time rings too.

** Change when it rings: SPC m R
On the task (or its line in the agenda) press =SPC m R= and answer:
- =30m=, =1h= or =1d=: that long before its time
- =8:30= or =tomorrow 9am=: at a time of its own, even for a task with no
  time at all
- =none=: never rings
- empty: back to the default, 10 minutes before

This is kept as the task's =:REMIND:= property.

** Setting a reminder from the phone
1. Tap *Agenda* on the home page.
2. Move to the task's line.
3. Press =SPC m R= (or =SPC m d s= to give it a time first).

A new task from the toolbar's *Add* rings once it is scheduled with a
time.  Reminders are written when an agenda file is saved on the phone,
and again every half hour, so tasks synced from the Mac ring too.  Only
the next 7 days are set.  Done tasks don't ring.

* Notes

** A new note: SPC n r f, then a new title
Type a title that doesn't exist and press RET.  The note asks:
1. What is it, in your own words?  (Empty answer: write it later.  The
   empty-notes walk will bring it back.)
2. Where is it from?  A link becomes its =ROAM_REFS=, and a source makes
   it a literature note.
3. Why does it matter to you?
4. What would you type to find it later?
5. What does it connect to?
A note made from an EHP note is tagged =ehp= too.

** Write for the search you'll make later
Don't just collect.  When you write a note, think: *when I need this in
six months, what will I type?*  Put those words in, whether or not the
title has them: a problem (=slow startup=), a person (=Subash=), a
place, or the moment you'll need it (=before an interview=).  Commas
separate them.  Words and tags you used before are offered.

They show, dimmed after a magnifier, in =SPC n r f= and =SPC n r i=,
and typing them finds the note.  They are kept as =:SEARCH:=.

** Give an old note search words: SPC n r w
In any note, =SPC n r w= adds words to find it by.  When you reread a
note (or write one in the empty-notes walk), you're asked too, if it
has none yet.

** Only the notes that have search words
In =SPC n r f=, type =has:search= to list only the notes with search
words.  =has:tags= does the same for notes with tags.  Add more words
after a space to narrow the list: =has:search guitar=.

** Link while you write: SPC n r i
=SPC n r i= inserts a link to a note.  A title that doesn't exist yet
makes a new note, which asks the same questions as =SPC n r f=.

** See what links here: SPC n r t
=SPC n r t= opens the side buffer: the notes that link to this one.  A
note with no links is easy to lose.  Link it from a note you'll visit.

** Another name for a note: SPC n r l a
An alias is a second title.  It shows as its own entry in =SPC n r f=,
and links can use it.  Use an alias for a true other name (an
abbreviation, a translation).  For anything else, use search words.

** Empty notes walk: SPC n r e
Goes through the notes with almost nothing in them, the most linked
first.  For each one:
- =w=: write a line or two
- =h=: a hub (its links are its content; never asked again)
- =d=: delete (only when nothing links to it)
- =n=: later
- =q=: stop
The daily review offers one a day.

** Break a big note down: SPC n r b
In a long note, =SPC n r b= goes through its headings.  Each one either
becomes its own note (a link is left in its place) or stays.  First it
asks for one line on what the whole note is about.  =SPC n r B= walks
all the big notes.  =k= keeps a note whole: a log or a list is never
asked again.

** Reread one note a day: SPC n r o
=SPC n r o= (or *Reread* on the phone's home page) opens today's note,
read-only: the most overdue one, or the one untouched longest.  In the
evening, the review asks what you'd keep from it now.  Your answer goes
under its =* Rereads= heading.  The next reread is a week away, then a
month, 3 months, 6 months, then a year.  A note you didn't open asks
nothing.

** Add a paper: SPC n r p
Paste a DOI or an arXiv id.  You get:
- its entry in =added.bib=
- its PDF
- a note with =ROAM_REFS= set
Zotero is not needed.

* Tasks and the day

** Every task gets a date
The daily review walks the tasks with no date (=SPC o v u= runs that
walk any time).  A task rescheduled a second time is offered a
breakdown into smaller steps (=SPC o v b=).

** Say how long a task takes: SPC m c E
Set an effort (=0:30=, =1:30=) on a task.  Time boxing gives the task a
block that long.  Without an effort, a task gets 45 minutes.

** Tomorrow in blocks: SPC o v T
The evening review ends with it, or press =SPC o v T= any time.  It
fits tomorrow's tasks around the things that already have a time,
highest priority first, between 5:00 and 23:00, with 15 minutes between
blocks.  Tasks that don't fit stay without a time.  Each block is a
scheduled time, so it rings.

** The calendar: SPC o c
It opens full screen.  On the phone it shows one day:
- =f= / =b=: next or previous day
- =.=: today
- =j=: go to a date
- =q=: close
The keys are also on the mode line.

** Move a block on the calendar
Long-press a block and drag it to move it.  Drag its edge to make it
longer or shorter.  A long-press without moving opens its menu.  With
keys:
- =n= / =p=: next or previous block
- =t=: give it a new time
- =d=: remove its time
- =RET=: open the task
- =i=: clock in

* Reviews

** The daily review: SPC o v t
On the phone it nags after 8 PM until the review is written.  In order,
it:
1. shows the day's facts and asks for your three lines
2. dates the undated tasks
3. asks what you'd keep from today's reread
4. offers one empty note and one big note
5. plans tomorrow's topics
6. puts tomorrow in blocks on the calendar
An empty answer skips; =q= stops a walk.

** The weekly review: SPC o v w
It opens once five of the week's seven dailies are written.  The
monthly review is =SPC o v m=.

#+TITLE: Voice, 1 to 100: our own engine, your words, the island
#+DATE: 2026-11-18

* 1. What changed
Before: the island asked the FUTO voice app to listen (its own screen in
the middle), took one string back, and showed what Emacs did as a toast.
The model was Phonon 2, pinned by the app, and it knew none of our words
("magit" came back "magic", "Hades 2" came back "head is too").

Now:
- *The engine is ours*: =hear= (voice-engine/), Phonon 2.5 in plain C,
  kept loaded in Termux, behind a Unix socket.  0.10 to 0.15 s for a
  sentence on the phone.  Same program on the Mac.
- *It knows your words*: a context graph over the model's word pieces
  pulls the decoder toward your tasks, apps, the commands' jargon and
  voice.org's names.  On synthetic speech: 11 of 33 right before, 29 of
  33 after, and 30 of 30 ordinary sentences unchanged.
- *The island is the interface*: it grows where it is into what it hears
  and then what was done; questions come down out of it to the middle of
  the screen and listen for the answer.  No second screen, no toasts.
- *Typing anywhere*: in another app's text field the words are typed
  (unless clearly a command); the keyboard's microphone key works too.
- *Every utterance is kept* with what was heard: the data for training
  the model on your voice every week.

#+begin_example
 long press / hold the island          the keyboard's mic key (VoiceIme)
        |  FloatService                          |
        +--> Hear.java: AudioRecord 16 kHz ------+
                 | PCM over a Unix socket (.emacs-home/hear.sock)
                 v
 hear serve (Termux, Phonon 2.5 loaded, P25_THREADS=6)
   finds speech start/end (energy, adaptive floor); decodes speculatively
   at 250 ms of quiet, so at the end (700 ms, or letting go) it is ready
   beam search + your words (vocab.txt, read again on change)
   -> START, PARTIAL words, END, TEXT best, ALT others; saves WAV + heard.tsv
                 |
 Hear.java -> island (bars, words)      keyboard: typed (IslandAccess)
          -> voice-heard.tsv: EPOCH TAB SOURCE TAB TEXT TAB ALT...
             emacsclient (voice-from-app): the reading that makes sense wins
             reply: quiet (did it) | front (a list to pick from) | type
          -> voice-said.tsv: the island says what was done
          -> voice-confirm.tsv: the question card, listening for yes or no
#+end_example

* 2. Measured
Phone: Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 (SM8735), 8 cores, ARMv9 with dot product and
i8mm, no SVE (the kernel turns it off), Android 17.  Engine built with
the NDK, =-march=armv8.2-a+dotprod=, run from adb's shell.

| Utterance (TTS) | Encoder | Decoder, plain | Decoder, your words | Plain | With your words |
|-----------------+---------+----------------+---------------------+-------+-----------------|
| open Hades 2 (Rishi) | 70 ms | 12 ms | 66 ms | Open head is too. | Open Hades 2. |
| run consult ripgrep (Samantha) | 91 ms | 13 ms | 58 ms | Run consult rib grab. | Run consult ripgrep. |
| open magit (Daniel) | 64 ms | 8 ms | 49 ms | Open magic. | Open Magit. |

- A whole process, model loading included: 0.34 s; kept loaded: 0.10 s.
  So a daemon, not a command per utterance.
- Threads: 6 best (4: slower decoder; 8: no better).
- The M5 Max does the same in 62 ms.
- Streaming test on the Mac (real-time audio, 0.5 s quiet, speech, quiet):
  END and TEXT together 0.5 s after the last word, =wait=0.000= — the
  speculative decode was ready.  Holding the island makes that 0.

Vocabulary, three voices (Samantha, Daniel, Rishi), 11 phrases, scored by
=voice-engine/test/check.sh= (=test/expect.tsv=, =test/negative.tsv=):

| Setting | Your words right | Ordinary sentences unchanged |
|---------+------------------+------------------------------|
| plain (greedy) | 11/33 | - |
| boost 1.5, flat | 20/33 | 30/30 |

* 3. Research (2026-11-18), with references
Three questions, researched in parallel; the full reports are summarised
here.

** Engines
- *phonon-2.5-c* is NEON with SDOT int8 dot products, a C library
  (=p25_engine_open=, =p25_transcribe=, stages exported), greedy TDT
  decoding in ~40 lines; =--stream= only emits per 25–35 s window, and
  there is no server.  [[https://github.com/eschmidbauer/phonon-2.5-c][github.com/eschmidbauer/phonon-2.5-c]] (MIT; weights
  CC-BY-4.0, [[https://huggingface.co/eschmidbauer/phonon-2.5-c][huggingface.co/eschmidbauer/phonon-2.5-c]]).  Phonon 2.5: 5.06 %
  average English WER vs Phonon 2's 5.21 %
  ([[https://huggingface.co/FermionResearch/Phonon-2.5][model card]]).
- *sherpa-onnx* runs in Termux (v2.0.1 tarballs), has Parakeet TDT
  0.6B v2/v3 and hotwords for NeMo transducers with modified beam search
  ([[https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/hotwords/index.html][hotwords]], [[https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3077][PR 3077]]); TDT beam search dropped speech until
  [[https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/4084][PR 4084]] (2026-10-27).  The reference for boost values; not needed.
- *Moonshine v2* streaming (Tiny/Small/Medium: 12.0 / 7.84 / 6.65 % WER)
  has key-term biasing built in, =boost·(1+ln depth)=; 98–180 ms after
  speech on an iPad ([[https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12241][arXiv 2602.12241]],
  [[https://moonshine-voice.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/domain-customization/][domain customisation]]).  The next model to try; its runtime in Termux
  is unverified.
- *whisper.cpp* steers with a prompt and GBNF grammars but is slow on
  phone CPUs for short audio ([[https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp/blob/master/examples/cli/README.md][CLI README]]).

** Biasing toward your words
- Shallow fusion with a biasing graph: [[https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2019/zhao19d_interspeech.html][Zhao et al. 2019]]; trie-based deep
  biasing: [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02194][Le et al. 2021]]; an Aho-Corasick context graph on icefall's:
  [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13514][arXiv 2409.13514]].
- NeMo's GPU-PB boosts RNN-T/TDT in greedy or beam, with depth scaling
  ([[https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html][NeMo word boosting]]); TurboBias notes flat weights work poorly in greedy
  ([[https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014v2][arXiv 2508.07014]]).
- What hear does (voice-engine/bias.c): sherpa-onnx's ContextGraph
  scoring (a bonus per piece, taken back if the phrase is left unfinished,
  kept when it completes), every spelling of a phrase in its fewest
  pieces or one more, deeper pieces boosted more, on a frame-synchronous
  beam search of 4 over the TDT decoder.  The tokenizer is multilingual
  and small: even "open" is two pieces, so "known words" can't be told
  from piece counts; Emacs sends only names and jargon instead (a word is
  jargon when it names a loaded Emacs library).

** Android, without being a keyboard
- Recording from the app (=AudioRecord=, =VOICE_RECOGNITION=, 16 kHz)
  inside the running service; audioserver silences background UIDs
  ([[https://developer.android.com/media/platform/sharing-audio-input][sharing audio input]]); an enabled accessibility service is bound with
  =BIND_INCLUDE_CAPABILITIES=, which carries the microphone capability to
  the app's processes ([[https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/android17-release/services/accessibility/java/com/android/server/accessibility/AccessibilityServiceConnection.java][AccessibilityServiceConnection]]).  Unverified on the
  phone; =dumpsys activity processes= should show =M=.
- Typing: an accessibility service with =flagInputMethodEditor= gets the
  focused field's input connection, as a keyboard does (Android 13,
  [[https://developer.android.com/reference/android/accessibilityservice/InputMethod][InputMethod]]); =commitText= inserts at the cursor.
- The keyboard's mic key: Unexpected Keyboard (and CleverKeys when its
  own engine is off) switch to an IME with a =voice= subtype
  ([[https://github.com/Julow/Unexpected-Keyboard/blob/master/srcs/juloo.keyboard2/VoiceImeSwitcher.java][VoiceImeSwitcher]], [[https://github.com/tribixbite/CleverKeys/blob/main/src/main/kotlin/tribixbite/cleverkeys/VoiceKeyRouting.kt][CleverKeys VoiceKeyRouting]]).  VoiceIme is that,
  shows nothing, and hands straight back.
- End of speech: Home Assistant's hangovers are 0.25 / 0.7 / 1.25 s
  ([[https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/homeassistant/components/assist_pipeline/vad.py][vad.py]]); FUTO's fork stops after ~1–3 s.  Hold to talk pays none.

** The apps you named
whisperIME (TFLite Whisper, RecognitionService, MIT), whisperIMEplus
(ONNX Whisper, GPLv3), the FUTO fork (many runtimes, glossary prompt,
audio history, source-first non-commercial), BiBi-Keyboard (sherpa-onnx,
accessibility floating ball, Apache-2.0), Dictate (FlorisBoard fork,
sherpa-onnx, Apache-2.0), Transcribro (whisper.cpp tiny, Silero VAD with
partials, ISC).  Taken: the engine out of process and kept warm, record
first and connect meanwhile, audio on disk with its transcript, a speech
gate, the typing route.  Not taken: model catalogues and downloaders,
benchmark UIs, cloud vendors, LLM cleanup, a keyboard.
Why Phonon 2.5 can't go into the FUTO app: it pins Phonon 2's sizes and
SHA-256s (=PhononModel.kt=), an install marker, and the native loader
refuses any format but =phonon-2-c-weights-v1=.

* 4. Training it on your voice, weekly
Every utterance is in Documents/voice-audio/heard (WAV + heard.tsv);
Emacs's voice log says what it was taken to mean, and a yes or a pick
confirms it.  The plan the research supports:
- Retrain only the prediction network and joint (17 MB of the model; the
  five-value encoder stays), from the base model each week on everything
  collected, with general speech mixed in so it forgets nothing
  ([[https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09251][Sim et al.]]: decoder-only fine-tuning on 50 utterances took name WER
  from 67 % to 55 %).  The encoder's outputs are computed once per
  utterance by hear; the TDT loss runs on the Mac's CPU in plain PyTorch
  ([[https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/loss/loss_tdt.py][transformers loss_tdt.py]]; NeMo's is CUDA only).
- Text-only adaptation of the prediction network from task names and
  commands ([[https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11127][Pylkkönen et al. 2021]]).
- Names that change (a new task, a new app) stay with the vocabulary: no
  training needed, the file changes and the engine reads it again.
